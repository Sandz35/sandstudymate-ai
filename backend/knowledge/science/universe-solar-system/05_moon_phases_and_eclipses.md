# The Moon, Phases, and Eclipses

## Learning Objectives
- Describe why the Moon has phases.
- Distinguish solar and lunar eclipses.
- Understand that the Moon reflects sunlight.

## Our Moon

The **Moon** is Earth’s natural satellite. It travels around Earth and reflects light from the Sun. The Moon does not make its own visible light.

## Why the Moon Has Phases

Half of the Moon is lit by the Sun at any one time (except during an eclipse). As the Moon moves around Earth, we see different portions of that sunlit half. The repeating pattern takes about **29.5 days**.

## Main Phases in Order

**New Moon → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Third (Last) Quarter → Waning Crescent → New Moon**. *Waxing* means the sunlit portion we see is growing; *waning* means it is shrinking.

## Solar Versus Lunar Eclipses

A **solar eclipse** happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth and blocks sunlight for some places on Earth. A **lunar eclipse** happens when Earth moves between the Sun and Moon and Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon. Eclipses do not happen every month because the Moon’s orbit is tilted.

## Safety

**Never look directly at the Sun** to observe an eclipse without proper solar-viewing protection. Ordinary sunglasses are not safe for direct solar viewing.

## Moon and Tides

The Moon’s gravity is an important cause of ocean tides; the Sun’s gravity also contributes.

## Key Vocabulary
- **satellite:** An object that orbits a larger object.
- **phase:** The changing appearance of the Moon’s sunlit part.
- **solar eclipse:** When the Moon blocks the Sun from view at certain places on Earth.
- **lunar eclipse:** When Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon.

## Common Misconceptions
- **Misconception:** Moon phases are caused by Earth’s shadow. **Correction:** Normal Moon phases come from our viewing angle of its sunlit half.
- **Misconception:** There must be an eclipse every full moon. **Correction:** The Moon’s orbit is tilted, so alignment is uncommon.

## Check Your Understanding

### Question 1
Does the Moon produce its own light?

**Answer:** No.
**Explanation:** The Moon reflects sunlight.

### Question 2
What is between the Sun and Earth during a solar eclipse?

**Answer:** The Moon.
**Explanation:** The Moon blocks sunlight along a narrow path.

## Summary
- Moon phases result from the Moon’s orbit and our changing view of sunlight.
- Solar and lunar eclipses are different alignments.
