# Classifying Living Things

## Learning Objectives
- Identify common levels of classification.
- Explain how classification groups related organisms.

## What Is Taxonomy?

**Taxonomy** is the science of naming and classifying living things. Scientists use physical features, DNA, and evolutionary relationships to organize organisms.

## Levels From Broad to Specific

A common sequence is **Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. Each step usually identifies a more specific group.

## Example: Humans

Humans belong to **Domain Eukarya**, **Kingdom Animalia**, **Phylum Chordata**, **Class Mammalia**, **Order Primates**, **Family Hominidae**, **Genus Homo**, **Species *Homo sapiens***.

## Three Domains

Scientists commonly recognize **Bacteria**, **Archaea**, and **Eukarya** as three domains. Plants, animals, fungi, and many other organisms belong to Eukarya.

## A Scientific Caution

Some organisms are difficult to classify neatly, and scientists may update groupings when new evidence becomes available.

## Key Vocabulary
- **taxonomy:** The science of naming and grouping organisms.
- **domain:** A very broad classification group.
- **genus:** A group of related species.
- **species:** A relatively specific classification group.

## Common Misconceptions
- **Misconception:** Classification never changes. **Correction:** Classifications may be revised when scientists learn more.
- **Misconception:** Genus is broader than kingdom. **Correction:** Kingdom is much broader than genus.

## Check Your Understanding

### Question 1
Which is broader: family or genus?

**Answer:** Family.
**Explanation:** A family may contain multiple genera.

### Question 2
What domain includes animals?

**Answer:** Eukarya.
**Explanation:** Animal cells have nuclei.

## Summary
- Taxonomy organizes living things from broad to specific groups.
- Scientific names help identify organisms consistently.
