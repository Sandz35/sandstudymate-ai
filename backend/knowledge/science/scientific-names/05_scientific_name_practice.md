# Scientific Names: Reading and Writing Practice

## Learning Objectives
- Correctly format simple scientific names.
- Identify the genus in a two-part name.

## Quick Rule

Write the **genus** first with a capital letter; write the **specific epithet** second in lowercase. Italicize both words in typed work. Example: *Felis catus*.

## Find the Genus

In *Panthera leo*, the genus is **Panthera**. In *Oryza sativa*, the genus is **Oryza**. In *Mangifera indica*, the genus is **Mangifera**.

## Fix the Mistakes

Incorrect: "panthera Leo". Correct: *Panthera leo*. Incorrect: "homo Sapiens". Correct: *Homo sapiens*. Incorrect: "solanum tuberosum" in plain formatting. Correct: *Solanum tuberosum* in italicized text.

## Same Genus, Different Species

*Panthera leo* and *Panthera tigris* share the genus but name different species. Sharing a genus does not mean the animals are identical.

## Study Strategy

Try five examples at a time. Say the everyday name, read the scientific name, then identify the genus. Check your spelling and capitalization.

## Key Vocabulary
- **italic:** Slanted text commonly used for a scientific species name.
- **epithet:** The second part of a two-word species name.
- **genus:** The first part of a two-word species name.

## Common Misconceptions
- **Misconception:** A scientific name can use either word order. **Correction:** The genus comes first.
- **Misconception:** Two animals in the same genus are automatically the same species. **Correction:** Different specific epithets can identify different species.

## Check Your Understanding

### Question 1
Correct this name: panthera Leo

**Answer:** *Panthera leo*.
**Explanation:** Capitalize the genus, lowercase the epithet, italicize both.

### Question 2
Which word is the genus in Solanum tuberosum?

**Answer:** *Solanum*.
**Explanation:** The genus is the first word.

## Summary
- Practice spelling, order, capitalization, and italics.
- Learn the meaning of the naming system, not just the examples.
