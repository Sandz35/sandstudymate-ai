# Why Do Living Things Have Scientific Names?

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why scientists use universal names.
- Identify the two parts of many scientific species names.

## Common Names Can Be Confusing

The same organism can have different common names in different places and languages. Scientists use a consistent naming system to help people around the world identify the same organism.

## Two-Part Species Names

In **binomial nomenclature**, a species name has two words: the **genus** and a **specific epithet**. For example, the human species is *Homo sapiens*. The complete species name is the two words together.

## Writing the Name Correctly

The genus begins with a **capital letter**; the specific epithet is **lowercase**. Both words are normally *italicized* in typed text. If writing by hand, underline both words separately. Example: *Panthera leo* (lion).

## Why Classification Matters

Scientists organize organisms into groups based on shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships. Classification helps compare species and communicate discoveries.

## A Useful Note

Scientific names can change when scientists discover new evidence. Different species can also share a genus name, such as *Panthera leo* and *Panthera tigris*.

## Key Vocabulary
- **common name:** An everyday name for an organism.
- **genus:** The first word in a two-part scientific species name.
- **specific epithet:** The second word in a two-part scientific species name.
- **binomial nomenclature:** The system of using a two-part species name.

## Common Misconceptions
- **Misconception:** The second word of a scientific name is capitalized. **Correction:** It is lowercase.
- **Misconception:** The scientific name is the same as the common name. **Correction:** Scientific names follow an internationally agreed system.

## Check Your Understanding

### Question 1
How many words are in a typical scientific species name?

**Answer:** Two.
**Explanation:** One for the genus and one specific epithet.

### Question 2
Which is written correctly: Homo Sapiens or Homo sapiens?

**Answer:** *Homo sapiens*.
**Explanation:** Only the genus begins with a capital letter.

## Summary
- Scientific names reduce confusion across languages.
- Two-part names use genus first, specific epithet second.
