# The Digestive System

## Learning Objectives
- Trace food through the digestive tract.
- Explain digestion and absorption.

## What Is Digestion?

**Digestion** breaks food into smaller substances that the body can absorb and use. It includes physical breakdown, such as chewing, and chemical breakdown by digestive substances.

## The Path Food Takes

**Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus.** Chewing starts digestion. The esophagus moves swallowed food to the stomach.

## Where Nutrients Are Absorbed

Most nutrient absorption happens in the **small intestine**. The **large intestine** absorbs water and helps form solid waste.

## Helper Organs

The **liver** makes bile that helps digest fats. The **gallbladder** stores bile. The **pancreas** supplies digestive enzymes and also produces hormones such as insulin.

## Healthy Habits

Eating a variety of foods, drinking water, and including fiber-rich foods can support normal digestion.

## Key Vocabulary
- **digestion:** Breaking food down into smaller substances.
- **esophagus:** Tube carrying swallowed food to the stomach.
- **small intestine:** Organ where most nutrient absorption happens.
- **large intestine:** Organ that absorbs water and prepares waste.

## Common Misconceptions
- **Misconception:** Most nutrients are absorbed in the stomach. **Correction:** Most nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine.
- **Misconception:** The liver stores bile. **Correction:** The liver makes bile; the gallbladder stores it.

## Check Your Understanding

### Question 1
Where are most nutrients absorbed?

**Answer:** Small intestine.
**Explanation:** Its lining absorbs nutrients.

### Question 2
What tube connects the mouth area to the stomach?

**Answer:** Esophagus.
**Explanation:** It moves swallowed food to the stomach.

## Summary
- The digestive tract breaks food down and absorbs nutrients.
- Different organs perform different steps.
