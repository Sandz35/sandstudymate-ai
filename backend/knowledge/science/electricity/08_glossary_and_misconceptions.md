# Electricity Vocabulary

## Glossary

- **Atom:** a tiny building block of matter.
- **Electron:** a negatively charged particle in an atom.
- **Proton:** a positively charged particle in an atom.
- **Electric charge:** a property of matter that causes electric forces.
- **Static electricity:** a buildup of electric charge on an object.
- **Electric current:** the flow of electric charge.
- **Circuit:** a path through which electric charge can flow.
- **Closed circuit:** a complete path that allows current to flow.
- **Open circuit:** an incomplete path that prevents current from flowing around the loop.
- **Conductor:** a material through which charge moves relatively easily.
- **Insulator:** a material that resists the movement of charge.
- **Battery:** a source that converts stored chemical energy into electrical energy.
- **Switch:** a device that opens or closes a circuit.
- **Series circuit:** a circuit with one path through all the components.
- **Parallel circuit:** a circuit with multiple branches.
- **Voltage:** a measure of electrical potential difference, which helps drive current; measured in volts (V).
- **Resistance:** how strongly a material or component opposes current; measured in ohms (Ω).
- **Power:** the rate at which energy is used or transferred; measured in watts (W).
- **Electromagnet:** a magnet whose field is produced by electric current.

## Common misconceptions corrected

**Misconception:** A battery contains electricity that gets used up inside the bulb.

**Correction:** A battery stores chemical energy. In a working circuit, it provides energy to move charge; the charge circulates rather than being used up like fuel.

**Misconception:** A circuit works as long as one wire touches the battery.

**Correction:** A simple bulb circuit requires a complete conducting loop between the two terminals.

**Misconception:** All materials are perfect conductors or perfect insulators.

**Correction:** Materials conduct to different degrees, and their behavior may change with conditions.

**Misconception:** In every parallel circuit, removing any bulb makes the others brighter.

**Correction:** In an ideal parallel circuit, removing one branch does not change the voltage across other branches. Real batteries and circuits can behave differently.

**Misconception:** Electricity and magnetism are unrelated.

**Correction:** Electric current makes magnetic fields, and changing magnetic fields can induce electric effects.
