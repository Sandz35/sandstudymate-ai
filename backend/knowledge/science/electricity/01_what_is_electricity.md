# What Is Electricity?

## Big idea

Electricity involves electric charges. Electric charges can build up in one place or move through a material. We use moving electric charges to run many devices.

## Electric charge

Matter is made of tiny particles called atoms. Atoms contain positively charged protons and negatively charged electrons. Objects can become electrically charged when they gain or lose electrons. Objects with the same kind of charge repel (push away); objects with opposite charges attract (pull toward each other).

## Static electricity

Static electricity is a buildup of electric charge on an object. Rubbing a balloon on dry hair can transfer electrons between the materials. The charged balloon may attract small paper pieces or make hair stand up. The charge is not continuously flowing around a complete circuit.

## Current electricity

Electric current is the flow of electric charge. In a simple metal-wire circuit, electrons carry the charge. A battery can provide the energy needed to move charge around a complete path. A light bulb uses electrical energy and changes it mainly into light and heat.

## Static versus current: comparison

| Static electricity | Current electricity |
|---|---|
| Charge builds up in a place | Charge flows through a path |
| Example: balloon sticks to wall | Example: battery lights a bulb |
| Often a short-lived effect | Can continue while the circuit works and has an energy source |

## Check your understanding

**Question:** Is a balloon sticking to hair an example of static electricity or current electricity?

**Answer:** Static electricity, because charge has built up on the balloon and hair.
