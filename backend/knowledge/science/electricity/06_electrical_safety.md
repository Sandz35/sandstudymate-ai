# Electrical Safety

## Why electricity safety matters

Electricity is useful, but household electrical power can cause serious shocks, burns, and fires. Children should never experiment with wall outlets or home wiring.

## Rules to remember

1. Keep hands and electrical devices away from water unless an adult says the device is designed for that use.
2. Never put objects into a wall outlet.
3. Do not touch torn cords, bare wires, sparks, or damaged plugs. Tell a trusted adult.
4. Ask an adult before plugging in or unplugging unfamiliar equipment; pull the plug body rather than the cord.
5. Keep batteries away from heat and fire. Never cut open, crush, or short-circuit a battery by connecting its two terminals directly with metal.
6. Use only teacher- or adult-approved low-voltage circuit kits for school projects.
7. Stay away from fallen power lines and tell an adult to call emergency services or the utility company.

## Why water increases danger

Ordinary water often contains dissolved minerals that let electrical current travel. Wet skin can also increase shock risk. Never test whether water conducts electricity using a wall outlet.

## What to do when something seems unsafe

Stop, move away, and alert a responsible adult. If someone is in contact with a live electrical source, do not touch the person or source; call emergency services and follow their directions.

## Check your understanding

**Question:** You see a frayed electrical cord near a sink. What should you do?

**Answer:** Do not touch or use it. Move away and tell an adult immediately.
