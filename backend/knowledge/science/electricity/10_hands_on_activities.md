# Safe Electricity Activities

## Activity 1: Balloon and paper pieces

**Question:** Can static electricity move paper without touching it?

**Materials:** Balloon, small dry paper pieces, dry hair or wool cloth.

**Steps:** (1) Tear paper into tiny pieces. (2) Rub the balloon on dry hair or wool. (3) Hold it close to, but not touching, the pieces. (4) Observe what happens.

**What to notice:** Some pieces may jump toward the balloon. Rubbing can transfer electrons, and electric forces can attract the paper.

**Safety:** Keep balloon pieces away from very young children and anyone with latex allergies; never use electrical outlets.

## Activity 2: Make a bulb glow

**Question:** What makes a closed circuit?

**Materials:** An adult-approved low-voltage battery holder, matching low-voltage bulb and holder, insulated wires, a classroom switch.

**Steps:** With adult help, connect battery → switch → bulb → back to battery. Close and open the switch. Observe the bulb.

**What to notice:** A closed switch completes the loop; an open switch breaks it.

**Safety:** Use only a low-voltage educational kit. Never connect the two battery terminals directly with a wire, and never use a household outlet.

## Activity 3: Test conductors and insulators

**Question:** Which materials complete a small circuit?

**Materials:** Same supervised low-voltage circuit, samples of aluminum foil, a metal paper clip, dry plastic, and dry wood.

**Steps:** With the switch open, have an adult set a small gap in the circuit. Place one test material in that gap, close the switch briefly, and record whether the lamp lights. Open the switch before changing samples.

**What to notice:** Metal samples often let enough current flow to light the bulb. Many dry nonmetals do not.

**Important limitation:** A bulb that stays dark does not prove zero current. Different material thicknesses and contact quality can change results.

## Activity 4: Predict series versus parallel

Draw two bulbs in one loop. Then draw two bulbs in separate branches. Predict what happens if one bulb is removed. Check your prediction using a prebuilt, adult-supervised low-voltage circuit board.

**Expected result:** Breaking the only path in series turns both off; in parallel, an intact other branch can stay lit.
