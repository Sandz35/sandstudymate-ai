# Conductors and Insulators

## Conductors

A **conductor** is a material that lets electric charge move through it relatively easily. Many metals, including copper and aluminum, are good conductors. Electrical wires often have a metal center to carry current.

## Insulators

An **insulator** is a material that strongly resists the movement of charge. Dry plastic, rubber, and glass are common examples. Wires often have plastic insulation on the outside to help protect people from accidental contact with the metal conductor.

## Everyday examples

| Object or material | Common classification | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Copper wire | Conductor | Carries current in many circuits |
| Aluminum foil | Conductor | Can complete a low-voltage test circuit |
| Dry plastic ruler | Insulator | Usually does not carry current easily |
| Rubber wire covering | Insulator | Helps keep conducting metal covered |
| Graphite (pencil lead) | Conducts electricity, but less well than copper | Can carry current in some experiments |

Materials are not perfectly either-or. Their behavior depends on conditions; for example, wet or dirty surfaces can be more conductive than dry ones.

## Safe classroom investigation

With an adult or teacher, use only a small battery-powered circuit and test clean, dry sample materials by placing them in a gap. If the bulb lights, the material may conduct enough current for that circuit. A bulb staying dark does **not** prove perfect insulation; the test circuit may simply be too weak to show a small current. Never connect experiments to household wall outlets.

## Check your understanding

**Question:** Why does a typical electrical wire have a metal center and plastic covering?

**Answer:** Metal conducts electricity, while the plastic covering helps prevent unwanted contact with the conducting metal.
