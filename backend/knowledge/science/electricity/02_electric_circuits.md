# Electric Circuits

## What is a circuit?

An electric circuit is a path that allows electric charge to flow. For a simple battery-powered bulb to light, the path must form an unbroken loop from one battery terminal, through the bulb, and back to the other terminal.

## Parts of a simple circuit

- **Battery (cell):** provides energy to push charges around the circuit.
- **Wire:** gives charge a conducting path.
- **Bulb (lamp):** changes electrical energy into light and heat.
- **Switch:** opens or closes the path to control the current.

## Closed circuit versus open circuit

A **closed circuit** has a complete conducting loop. Current can flow and a working bulb can glow.

An **open circuit** has a break, such as an open switch or disconnected wire. Current cannot flow around the complete loop, so the bulb is off.

## Easy-to-read circuit paths

Closed: `battery (+) → closed switch → bulb → battery (−)` (all connected in a loop)

Open: `battery (+) → open switch  // gap //  bulb → battery (−)` (broken path)

The arrows above describe the conventional current direction used in diagrams; electrons in a metal wire drift in the opposite direction.

## Why do two battery connections matter?

A bulb connected to only one battery terminal usually will not light because the circuit is incomplete. The bulb needs connections that form a complete path between the battery's two terminals.

## Check your understanding

**Question:** A bulb does not light because a switch is open. What should you do?

**Answer:** Close the switch to complete the circuit, assuming the battery and bulb work and the connections are correct.
