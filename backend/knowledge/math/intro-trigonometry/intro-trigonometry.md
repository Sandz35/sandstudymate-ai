# Introduction to trigonometry (right triangles)
## Right triangles
A right triangle has one 90-degree angle, the square corner. The longest side, across
from the right angle, is the hypotenuse. The angles in any triangle add up to 180
degrees, so the other two angles of a right triangle add up to 90 degrees.
## Naming the sides from an angle
Pick one of the two smaller angles. The side next to it (not the hypotenuse) is the
adjacent side. The side across from it is the opposite side.
## Sine, cosine and tangent are ratios
Trigonometry compares side lengths. Sine = opposite / hypotenuse. Cosine = adjacent /
hypotenuse. Tangent = opposite / adjacent. A memory trick is SOH-CAH-TOA.
## A starter example
In a right triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5, the hypotenuse is 5. For the angle across
from the side of length 3: sine = 3/5, cosine = 4/5, tangent = 3/4.
## Good first questions for beginners
Find the hypotenuse, find a missing angle using the 180-degree rule, or compute one
ratio from given side lengths.
